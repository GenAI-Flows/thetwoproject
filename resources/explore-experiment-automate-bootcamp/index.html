Explore, Experiment, Automate: A T-W-O Project Bootcamp

Posted: October 29, 2026 | Wei Chen | 1:44

See how a real business problem becomes an AI workflow through exploration, step-by-step testing, and automation with human review. This short explainer follows a daily portfolio-brief example.

Key takeaways

  • Start with a real problem and explore where AI helps—and where it fails.
  • Break the workflow into steps, check each answer, and fix weak steps before connecting them.
  • Chain tested skills into an automation with a trigger, a useful output, and a human review checkpoint.
  • Use the example to identify which of your team's own workflows is worth testing next.

Start with work, not a tool

A useful AI experiment starts with a problem someone owns. In this short bootcamp explainer, the problem is a retail investor's morning: the portfolio moved overnight, but news, sentiment, and earnings still need to be turned into an understandable brief.

The example is a learning exercise in workflow design, not investment advice. The important questions are what AI can handle, where its answers need checking, and who reviews the result.

Explore what AI can really do

Before designing an automation, try the individual tasks. Ask AI to summarize a headline, read an earnings call, or assess market sentiment. Compare useful answers with weak ones. Exploration reveals both possibilities and limits; a confident answer is not the same as a reliable one.

Experiment one step at a time

Break the morning brief into a sequence: get the portfolio, check relevant news, assess sentiment, review fundamentals, and draft a recommendation for human review. Test each step separately. When a step stumbles, fix it and run it again before relying on its output elsewhere.

This makes the workflow inspectable. The team can see where an answer came from and decide what evidence or judgment is still needed.

Automate with ownership intact

Once the steps work, package them as reusable skills and connect their inputs and outputs. Add a trigger, such as a daily morning run, and deliver the result in a useful format, such as a PDF brief or slide deck.

Keep a human review checkpoint before anyone acts on the recommendation. Automation changes the sequence of work; it does not remove responsibility for the decision.

Bring your team's problem

The portfolio brief is one example. Your starting point might be a recurring report, a handoff, or a daily review. Choose a workflow with a clear owner and a result the team can evaluate.
